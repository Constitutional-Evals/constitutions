# Foundational documents for the `environmental_ethics` anchor

Source texts the `environmental_ethics` constitution is distilled from. The bundle combines Aldo Leopold's land ethic, J. Baird Callicott's philosophical interpretation of that ethic, Arne Næss's deep ecology framework, and Holmes Rolston III's broader account of nonhuman value and duties toward the natural world.

## Source coverage

### Aldo Leopold — *The Land Ethic*

Leopold provides the core ecocentric framework of the corpus. He expands the moral community to include soils, waters, plants, animals, and the biotic community, emphasizing ecological interdependence, land health, responsibility, and the inadequacy of treating nature solely according to economic value.

### J. Baird Callicott — *The Conceptual Foundations of the Land Ethic*

Callicott provides the principal philosophical interpretation of Leopold. He connects the land ethic to moral sentiment, evolutionary accounts of cooperation, and ecological community, while explaining how concern for the biotic whole can coexist with previously established moral duties toward humans.

### Arne Næss — *The Shallow and the Deep*

Næss contributes the deep ecology perspective. He emphasizes the relational character of ecological life, biospherical egalitarianism, diversity, symbiosis, complexity, resistance to domination, local autonomy, decentralization, and the need to move beyond environmental policies concerned only with human health or resource availability.

### Holmes Rolston III — *Environmental Ethics: Values in and Duties to the Natural World*

Rolston broadens the corpus into a more systematic theory of environmental value. He examines the moral significance of animals, organisms, species, ecosystems, and natural processes, challenging the assumption that value exists only in relation to human preferences or experiences.

## Corpus perspective

Together, the sources present environmental ethics as an extension of moral concern beyond narrowly human interests. The bundle emphasizes **ecological community, intrinsic and systemic value, interdependence, biodiversity, respect for nonhuman life, limits on domination, ecological responsibility, and preservation of the integrity and functioning of natural systems**.

## Licensing and redistribution

All four files are used as **internal research sources** and are marked `redistributable: false`. They may remain part of the private constitution-generation corpus but should not be included when the foundational-source repository is made public.

The Næss source explicitly permits research, teaching, and private study while restricting redistribution. The Leopold, Callicott, and Rolston materials are likewise treated conservatively as copyrighted because the source copies used for this corpus do not provide an open redistribution license.

The README and manifest should remain in the public repository even when the underlying source files are excluded, so that the provenance and intellectual basis of the generated constitution remain documented.
