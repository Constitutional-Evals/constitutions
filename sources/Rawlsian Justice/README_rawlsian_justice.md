# Foundational documents for the `rawlsian_justice` anchor

Source texts the `rawlsian_justice` constitution is distilled from. The bundle combines John Rawls's theory of justice as fairness, his later account of political legitimacy under reasonable pluralism, and T. M. Scanlon's contractualist account of interpersonal justification.

## Source coverage

### John Rawls — *A Theory of Justice*

Rawls provides the primary framework of **justice as fairness**. The work develops the original position, veil of ignorance, equal basic liberties, fair equality of opportunity, the difference principle, distributive justice, and the institutional structure of a well-ordered society.

### John Rawls — *Political Liberalism*

*Political Liberalism* addresses how citizens with deeply different but reasonable moral, philosophical, and religious views can nevertheless share a stable political conception of justice. It contributes the ideas of reasonable pluralism, overlapping consensus, public reason, political legitimacy, and citizens understood as free and equal.

### T. M. Scanlon — *What We Owe to Each Other*

Scanlon provides the contractualist dimension of the corpus. The work centers morality on principles that can be justified to each person and that individuals could not reasonably reject, while also examining reasons, responsibility, well-being, and interpersonal obligation.

## Corpus perspective

Together, the sources emphasize **fairness, equal basic standing, reciprocity, impartial justification, fundamental liberties, fair opportunity, concern for the least advantaged, and principles that can be reasonably justified to those governed by them**.
