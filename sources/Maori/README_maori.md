# Foundational documents for the `maori` anchor

Source texts the `maori` constitution is distilled from. The bundle combines Māori Marsden's holistic account of kaitiakitanga, Merata Kawharu's kin-based socio-environmental account, and two New Zealand Law Commission sources that broaden the corpus across tikanga Māori, Māori custom law, and contemporary understandings of core values and principles.

## Source coverage

### Māori Marsden — *Kaitiakitanga*

Marsden provides the holistic philosophical foundation of the corpus. He situates kaitiakitanga within Māori cosmology, whakapapa, mauri, tapu, tikanga, relationships with Papatūānuku, and an integrated spiritual and material understanding of the world, showing why kaitiakitanga cannot be reduced to a narrow idea of environmental guardianship.

### Merata Kawharu — *Kaitiakitanga*

Kawharu develops kaitiakitanga as both a **social and environmental ethic** embedded in kinship and whakapapa. She connects it to rangatiratanga, mana whenua, mauri, tapu, rāhui, manaakitanga, reciprocity, obligation, and responsibilities extending among ancestors, living communities, future generations, and the environment.

### *Māori Custom and Values in New Zealand Law* — NZLC SP9

The 2001 Law Commission study broadens the corpus beyond kaitiakitanga to the underlying values of tikanga Māori and Māori custom law. It emphasizes that tikanga is living and adaptive rather than frozen in the past, and covers values and relationships including whakapapa, whanaungatanga, mana, tapu and noa, utu, kotahitanga, rangatiratanga, communal decision-making, and the relationship between tikanga and state law.

### *Appendix 1: Tikanga* — NZLC SP24

The 2023 appendix provides the most contemporary systematic account in the bundle. It explains tikanga within te ao Māori, mātauranga Māori, and mātauranga ā-iwi and covers concepts including whakapapa, whanaungatanga, mauri, mana, mana whenua, tapu, noa, utu, manaakitanga, kaitiaki, rāhui, kotahitanga, kawa, and reconciliation.

## Corpus perspective

Together, the sources emphasize **whakapapa and relationality, tikanga, kaitiakitanga, mana and rangatiratanga, reciprocal obligations, manaakitanga, mauri, tapu and noa, responsibilities to land and resources, kinship and community, continuity between ancestors, present people and future generations, and the maintenance or restoration of balanced relationships**.

This is intentionally a **plural corpus**, not a claim that all Māori communities apply tikanga identically. Tikanga operates through enduring values and principles, but their application can differ among iwi, hapū, whānau, and particular places. The 2023 Law Commission appendix makes this distinction especially explicit by separating broad mātauranga Māori from its contextual application through mātauranga ā-iwi.
