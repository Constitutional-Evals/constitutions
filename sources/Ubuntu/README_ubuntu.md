# Foundational documents for the `ubuntu` anchor

Source texts the `ubuntu` constitution is distilled from. The bundle combines Mogobe B. Ramose's philosophical account of ubuntu, Thaddeus Metz's systematic normative reconstruction, Desmond Tutu's application of ubuntu to forgiveness and reconciliation, and Ewuoso and Hall's systematic review of common themes across the wider ubuntu literature.

## Source coverage

### Mogobe B. Ramose — *The Relevance of Ubuntu to the Study of Philosophy*

Ramose provides the philosophical and ontological grounding of the corpus. He treats ubuntu as **humanness** rather than a fixed “-ism,” connecting it to *umuntu*, be-ing and becoming, relational personhood, openness to change, and an ethics of mutual care, recognition, sharing, and life with others.

### Thaddeus Metz — *Toward an African Moral Theory*

Metz develops ubuntu into a more explicit theory of right action. His account centers on harmonious relationships characterized by **identity and solidarity**, and uses this framework to explain values such as reconciliation, consensus, cooperation, aid to others, and community.

### Desmond Tutu — *No Future Without Forgiveness*

Tutu supplies the practical and restorative dimension of the corpus. Through South Africa's transition and the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, he connects ubuntu to forgiveness, reconciliation, communal harmony, rejection of retaliation, and the possibility of restoring relationships after serious wrongdoing.

### C. Ewuoso and S. Hall — *Core Aspects of Ubuntu*

Ewuoso and Hall serve as the broad interpretive and coverage check for the anchor. Their systematic review finds recurring emphasis across ubuntu scholarship on relationality, interdependence, fellowship, reconciliation, compassion, community friendliness, harmonious relationships, and other-regarding action.

## Corpus perspective

Together, the sources emphasize **relational personhood, human interdependence, identity and solidarity, compassion, mutual care, community, reconciliation, forgiveness, consensus, shared flourishing, and the restoration and preservation of harmonious relationships**.
