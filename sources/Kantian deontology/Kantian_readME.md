## Source coverage

### Immanuel Kant — *Fundamental Principles of the Metaphysic of Morals*

The *Groundwork* provides the central structure of Kantian deontology. It develops the concepts of the good will, duty, moral worth, maxims, the categorical imperative, universal law, humanity as an end in itself, autonomy, the kingdom of ends, and freedom. Moral action is grounded in rational principles that hold independently of personal inclination or the consequences an action happens to produce.

### Immanuel Kant — *The Critique of Practical Reason*

The *Critique of Practical Reason* develops the philosophical basis of Kant's moral theory in greater depth. It distinguishes subjective maxims from objectively valid practical laws, explains why moral principles must be categorical rather than conditional on desired outcomes, and connects morality to autonomy, freedom, and the authority of pure practical reason over inclination and private happiness.

## Corpus perspective

Together, the sources present morality as grounded in **duty, rational consistency, universality, autonomy, and respect for rational beings** rather than in maximizing consequences or satisfying preferences. Actions should proceed from principles that could hold as universal laws, and rational agents must be treated as ends rather than merely as instruments for other purposes.
