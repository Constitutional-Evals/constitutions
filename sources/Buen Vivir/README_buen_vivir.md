# Foundational documents for the `buen_vivir` anchor

Source texts the `buen_vivir` constitution is distilled from. The bundle combines Alberto Acosta's and Eduardo Gudynas's philosophical and political accounts of Buen Vivir with its constitutional expression in Ecuador and Bolivia.

## Source coverage

### Alberto Acosta — *Rethinking the World from the Perspective of Buen Vivir*

Acosta presents Buen Vivir as a plural and evolving project of **living well together** rather than a single model of the good life. He emphasizes community, reciprocity, solidarity, decolonization, harmony with nature, post-extractivism, participatory self-government, and rejection of endless economic growth as the measure of human progress.

### Eduardo Gudynas — *Buen Vivir: Today's Tomorrow*

Gudynas develops Buen Vivir as a plural platform for **alternatives to development**. He emphasizes indigenous contributions, interculturality, community-based well-being, intrinsic values in nature, dissolution of the society-nature divide, decolonization, and rejection of linear, Eurocentric, and purely economic understandings of development.

### Ecuador — Constitution of 2008

The Ecuadorian Constitution gives Buen Vivir, or **sumak kawsay**, an institutional and rights-based form. The selected passages connect the good way of living to water, food, health, education, housing, work, social security, participation, cultural pluralism, sustainability, and the rights of nature.

### Bolivia — Constitution of 2009

The Bolivian Constitution treats **Vivir Bien** and related indigenous concepts as ethical and political principles of the plurinational state. It emphasizes reciprocity, solidarity, interdependence, harmony, social justice, communal democracy, indigenous autonomy, collective welfare, pluralism, and responsibility toward present and future generations.

## Corpus perspective

Together, the sources emphasize **community and reciprocity, plural ways of living well, decolonization, interculturality, social and ecological harmony, collective welfare, participatory governance, rights and responsibilities, limits to extractivism and growth-centered development, and a relationship with nature that exceeds purely instrumental or economic valuation**.

## Licensing and redistribution

The Acosta and Gudynas texts are treated as `redistributable: false` because no open redistribution license was established for the copies used in this corpus. The Bolivian constitutional file is also `redistributable: false` because its English translation explicitly states that the translation is copyrighted and all rights are reserved.

The Ecuadorian constitutional extract is treated as `redistributable: true` as an official legal text and official translation. The manifest and README may remain public regardless of whether the underlying non-redistributable files are included.
