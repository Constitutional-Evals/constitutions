## Source coverage

### John Stuart Mill — *Utilitarianism*

Mill provides the core statement of the utilitarian framework. He defines right action in terms of promoting happiness, develops the distinction between higher and lower pleasures, emphasizes the greatest overall happiness rather than the agent's private welfare, and explains how justice, rights, moral rules, and social obligations can be understood through utility.

### Henry Sidgwick — *The Methods of Ethics*

Sidgwick provides a more systematic account of utilitarianism as **Universalistic Hedonism**. The selected material examines impartial concern for the happiness of all sentient beings, the tension between egoism and general happiness, justice and equality, and the relationship between common-sense moral rules and the ultimate utilitarian standard.

## Corpus perspective

Together, the sources present morality as centered on **impartial promotion of overall well-being**. The corpus emphasizes consequences for happiness and suffering, equal consideration of comparable interests, the importance of higher forms of human flourishing, and the use of moral rules and institutions insofar as they reliably contribute to general welfare.
