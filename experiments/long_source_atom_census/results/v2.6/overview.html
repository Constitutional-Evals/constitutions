What we actually tested, and what the 10-hour run taught us

This experiment tested whether a complete normative census could be compressed before constitution writing without losing important content. It did not test whether MoReBench-inspired prompting improves constitution generation or review.

Tested

Post-census selection: 40 reference clusters versus a 21-cluster, approximately 50% evidence packet.

Not tested

Conventional prompts versus MoReBench-informed generator/reviewer prompts.

01 / Design

The causal question

Holding writer, schema, seeds, and evaluation machinery constant, does a selected cluster packet preserve the normative coverage and pairwise judgments produced from the complete cluster packet?

02 / Result

03 / Findings

What was useful

The selector failed, but the run established several engineering facts and exposed measurement problems before they reached a publication claim.

Positive findings

  • Exhaustive chunk traversal worked Every source chunk was processed by both extractors, avoiding premature passage filtering.
  • Grounding was strong Exact-evidence acceptance was 91.1%, 79.4%, and 86.7%; no condition had reviewer-consensus unsupported items.
  • Deterministic clustering was complete Each tradition produced a complete, non-overlapping 40-cluster partition.
  • Compression was real The writer saw approximately half the exhaustive evidence, while every high-confidence conflict or exception survived.
  • Reproducible artifacts exist All 45 candidates, 135 reviews, 45 rankings, configs, source hashes, and preregistered gates completed without optional stopping.

Negative findings

  • Half the packet was too aggressive Coverage noninferiority failed in every tradition, with the largest loss in Christianity.
  • Behavioral preservation did not replicate Christianity reached only 0.75 agreement with exhaustive consensus, below the 0.90 gate.
  • Simple truncation remained competitive on probes It matched or exceeded budgeted probe accuracy in all three traditions, weakening the case for added complexity.
  • Five seeds yielded coarse uncertainty Twenty-point reviewer steps dominated paired differences and produced low-resolution intervals.
  • This does not answer the MoReBench question MoReBench-inspired review ideas were held constant; no conventional-versus-MoReBench ablation was run.
04 / Audit

Why the evaluation is not final

These issues were identified after collection. They do not rescue the failed selector; they limit how much the behavioral and scalar review results can support.

Leakage

Held-out files were unused

Probes came from the same 40 reference clusters supplied to writers. Accuracy measures internal consistency, not unseen-source generalization.

Calibration

Reviewers used incompatible scales

Coverage rank correlations were weak and sometimes negative. Median aggregation often made Qwen's coarse 45/65/85 scale decisive.

Ceiling

Most probes were too easy

Every condition reached median accuracy 1.0 for Stoicism and Lockean rights, so these cases could not separate methods.

Contract

Label balance was not enforced

Christianity produced 2 A versus 10 B targets and Stoicism 9 versus 3, despite a prompt requesting balance.

05 / Interpretation

Claims we can and cannot make

The useful output is a precise boundary around the evidence, not a favorable narrative assembled after the fact.

Supported

  • Exhaustive atom extraction and deterministic clustering are technically feasible on the Spark.
  • A 50% cluster packet can preserve grounding and explicit conflict clusters.
  • The tested selector did not preserve preregistered coverage.
  • The evaluation design exposed concrete issues worth fixing.

Not supported

  • The 50% selector is noninferior to the complete packet.
  • Generated probes demonstrate unseen-source generalization.
  • The constitutions are discriminative across moral traditions.
  • MoReBench-style generation or review improves EigenBench.
06 / Next

Do the cheap question first

Before another long-source run, isolate the question that originally motivated the work: whether MoReBench-informed construction and review prompts improve constitutions.

Next experiment

MoReBench prompt ablation

Hold sources, model, seeds, schema, and cases constant. Compare conventional generation/review against a MoReBench-informed generator and full diagnostic reviewer. This directly tests the intended contribution at a fraction of the cost.

Only if source selection remains a priority

Long-source v3

Rebuild the selection study around truly held-out sources, expert-validated hard cases, calibrated item-to-cluster coverage, token-matched baselines, multiple pair judges, and cross-theory discrimination.

v3 source-selection protocol

01 / Split

Prevent leakage

Freeze disjoint development, writer-primary, evaluation-source, evaluation-case, and final-tradition partitions.

02 / Match

Compare fair budgets

Use tokenizer-measured oracle, hierarchical, raw-prefix, stratified-raw, and summary conditions.

03 / Calibrate

Replace scalar coverage

Use expert-calibrated item-to-cluster labels with reliability and adjudication reported.

04 / Challenge

Use hard held-out cases

At least 50 cases per tradition, multiple blinded judges, balanced positions, and expert targets.

05 / Discriminate

Test neighboring theories

Report cross-theory margins and confusion matrices on conflicts where traditions predict different choices.
